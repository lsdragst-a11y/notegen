# Product

## Register

product

## Users

NoteGen is for learners, students, researchers, and knowledge workers who turn long-form video into material they can review, search, cite, and ask questions about. They may import online courses, technical tutorials, lectures, interviews, or local video files, then work inside a focused notebook-style workspace.

The primary user context is task-oriented study: watching, reading generated notes, jumping between citations and source video, asking follow-up questions, exporting material, and tracking learning progress.

## Product Purpose

NoteGen converts video into structured learning notes with chapters, key points, transcript evidence, bilingual output, export, bookmarks, sharing, and grounded Q&A. Success means the user can move from an unstructured video to a trustworthy, reviewable notebook without losing the link back to the original source.

The product should make three relationships obvious:

- video time becomes chapter structure
- generated notes remain grounded in source evidence
- questions and answers can jump back to exact timestamps

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, warm, precise.

The brand language is "Folding Cinema": video time is folded into notebook pages. Marketing surfaces can feel more cinematic and memorable; workspace surfaces must remain calm, efficient, and familiar enough for sustained study.

Authentication and onboarding can feel friendly through the paper-page companion, but the product must avoid childish cartoon treatment. The companion is a personified note object, not a human mascot.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI SaaS visuals: purple glow gradients, particle backgrounds, decorative looping motion, glass cards everywhere, and identical three-card grids.

Avoid turning the workspace into a cinematic scroll experience. Once the user is studying, motion should express state, feedback, evidence tracing, or progress only.

Avoid hiding content behind hover-only interactions or making citation/evidence affordances depend on color alone.

## Design Principles

1. Show the source trail. Every important generated answer or note should keep a visible path back to the video evidence.
2. Make motion explain the product. The signature interaction is video folding into notes; workspace motion is for locating, saving, processing, or revealing evidence.
3. Keep study surfaces quiet. The notebook workspace should prioritize reading, keyboard use, video seek, and Q&A speed.
4. Let the brand live at entrances and transitions. Homepage, auth, and generation can carry more personality than dense product screens.
5. Preserve trust under failure. Queueing, processing, errors, and retries need calm, legible states rather than theatrical shakes or fake progress.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target accessible, keyboard-operable product UI with clear focus states and readable contrast. Reduced-motion support is mandatory for homepage motion, auth companion behavior, citation tracing, generation progress, and any future fold/lens interactions.

Mobile must keep the core task available without relying on hover. Complex desktop motion should collapse to static or locally highlighted states on touch devices.
